# Sender+ Website Brand Guide

## 1. Brand essence

Sender+ is a Ghanaian technology-enabled package delivery company.

The website should feel like the meeting point of:

- premium consumer technology
- modern logistics
- Ghanaian warmth and local understanding

The visual identity must communicate confidence and trust without becoming stiff or corporate.

## 2. Brand proposition

Customer-facing proposition:

> Send anywhere in your region.

Tagline:

> Bridging Ghana One Package at a Time.

The proposition explains the service. The tagline expresses the broader brand mission.

## 3. Brand personality

Sender+ should feel:

- clear
- confident
- warm
- efficient
- secure
- visible
- modern
- thoughtful
- distinctly Ghanaian without relying on clichés
- human rather than robotic

Avoid a voice that feels:

- inflated
- bureaucratic
- overly technical
- childish
- slang-heavy
- generic startup jargon
- excessively corporate

## 4. Color palette

### Primary

**Sender Blue / Teal**

`#77C4E0`

Role:

- innovation
- visibility
- clarity
- friendliness
- technology

Use intentionally for major accents, selected backgrounds, interactive states, or brand moments.

**Sender Deep Red**

`#B22222`

Role:

- energy
- urgency
- strength
- confidence
- operational decisiveness

Use more sparingly than the blue. It should create emphasis, not visual noise.

### Neutrals

Build a refined neutral system around:

- near-black
- white
- warm off-white
- light gray
- medium gray
- charcoal

Prefer slightly warm neutrals where they complement package photography and human imagery.

### Cardboard brown

Natural cardboard tones are part of the physical-delivery visual world.

Prefer them through:

- package photography
- subtle background details
- tactile accents

Do not turn cardboard brown into a third dominant digital brand color.

## 5. Color usage principles

Most pages should breathe through neutral space.

Do not use both primary colors at full intensity in every section.

Recommended approach:

- neutral page canvas
- one dominant accent per composition
- occasional deep-red emphasis
- teal for visual continuity and interactive recognition

Avoid:

- red/blue gradients everywhere
- neon interpretations of brand colors
- low-contrast pale-teal body text
- red body copy
- decorative color with no hierarchy purpose

## 6. Typography

Use a modern sans-serif system optimized through Next.js.

### Display typography

Should feel:

- bold
- clean
- geometric or contemporary
- confident at large sizes
- professional rather than playful

Use large type confidently on hero and editorial sections.

Avoid excessive all-caps headings.

### Body typography

Should be:

- highly legible
- comfortable on mobile
- restrained in width
- generous enough in line-height to feel premium

### Suggested hierarchy

Establish tokens/styles for:

- Display
- H1
- H2
- H3
- Body Large
- Body
- Small
- Eyebrow / Metadata

Avoid randomly assigning font sizes in individual sections.

## 7. Layout and spacing

The site should feel spacious and composed.

Use:

- generous section spacing
- controlled content widths
- asymmetric editorial layouts where appropriate
- deliberate negative space
- strong rhythm between image-heavy and text-heavy moments

Do not make every section a centered stack of heading + paragraph + three cards.

Major audience and brand-story sections should feel like designed compositions.

## 8. Shape language

Use rounded corners selectively.

Recommended:

- buttons: modest-to-medium radius
- selected images: restrained radius where composition benefits
- panels: subtle radius

Avoid:

- pill-shaped everything
- every section inside a rounded card
- nested rounded containers
- large bubble-like SaaS UI

## 9. Buttons and CTAs

Primary CTA should feel decisive.

Primary recurring CTA:

> Send a Package

Secondary recurring CTA:

> Track a Package

Button qualities:

- strong contrast
- clear hover/focus behavior
- comfortable touch target
- concise labels
- no excessive shadows

Use text links where a full button would make a composition too heavy.

## 10. Photography

Photography is central to making Sender+ feel real rather than abstract.

Preferred imagery:

- Ghanaian people
- actual packages
- delivery staff
- branded vehicles
- campuses when relevant
- local commercial environments
- natural daylight
- believable logistics moments

Avoid generic stock imagery that could belong to any courier company in any country.

When real Sender+ photography exists, prioritize it.

### Hero treatment

The supplied hero image with a Sender+ representative, package, and branded van should be treated as a major brand asset.

Do not simply place it in a generic rectangular card.

Explore:

- art-directed crop
- edge-to-edge image areas
- layered text/image composition
- controlled masks
- selective overlays for legibility
- responsive focal positioning

Never distort the image.

## 11. Logo usage

Use the provided Sender+ logo without altering its proportions.

Do not:

- stretch it
- recolor it arbitrarily
- add drop shadows
- place it on visually noisy backgrounds without adequate contrast
- redraw it with text unless explicitly required

Give it sufficient clear space.

Use the logo as a brand identifier, not as a decorative texture.

## 12. Iconography

Use icons sparingly and consistently.

Prefer:

- simple line icons
- a single coherent icon family
- icons that clarify function

Avoid:

- icons on every paragraph
- emoji-style service visuals
- mixing multiple icon libraries
- decorative icons without information value

## 13. Motion

Motion should signal polish, not spectacle.

Appropriate uses:

- subtle hero entrance
- image reveal
- navigation transition
- button interaction
- refined hover states
- restrained route or movement visualization
- section entrance where it improves rhythm

Avoid:

- constant floating elements
- heavy parallax
- long blocking intro animations
- excessive scroll-triggered effects
- autoplay distractions

Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 14. Audience-specific expression

### Students

The Students page may be slightly more energetic and conversational, but it must remain within the premium brand system.

Do not make it look like a separate youth campaign microsite.

### Business

The Business page should feel commercially confident and efficient.

Avoid enterprise-software clichés such as dashboards everywhere, meaningless charts, and generic “scale your business” jargon.

### Everyday senders

The core site must stay simple enough that an individual sender understands the service immediately.

## 15. Ghanaian identity

Express Ghanaian identity through:

- real local photography
- grounded language
- local use cases
- local context
- human service
- the company story

Avoid relying on:

- flag motifs everywhere
- kente patterns as generic decoration
- clichés about African markets
- exaggerated cultural symbolism

The brand should feel Ghanaian because it understands Ghana, not because every surface is decorated with national motifs.

## 16. Brand copy principles

Prefer:

- short statements
- active language
- specific benefit
- human phrasing

Examples of approved direction:

> Your package. Anywhere in your region. Delivered.

> Campus life is busy enough.

> You sell it. We deliver it.

> Built in Ghana. Built for how Ghana moves.

> Ready to send?

Avoid:

- “revolutionizing the logistics ecosystem”
- “cutting-edge innovative solutions”
- “seamless end-to-end synergistic platform”
- unsupported superlatives such as “Ghana’s #1”

## 17. ACCESS values

Sender+ values form ACCESS:

- Availability
- Customer Care
- Excellence
- Speed
- Security

The website may explain ACCESS on the About page.

Elsewhere, translate the values into tangible experiences:

- visibility and status awareness
- responsive support
- careful package handling
- efficient movement
- verification and secure handoff

Do not overuse the acronym in sales copy.

## 18. Visual anti-pattern checklist

Before approving a new page or section, check that it does not rely on:

- five or more identical cards in a row
- oversized gradient blobs
- glowing borders
- fake app dashboards
- fake testimonials
- fake metrics
- giant icon grids
- decorative maps with invented routes
- three consecutive centered sections
- excessive shadows
- excessive border lines
- overly rounded containers
- generic SaaS screenshots
- unverified operational promises

## 19. Quality test

A successful Sender+ page should feel recognizable even if the logo were temporarily hidden.

That recognition should come from:

- typography
- color restraint
- photography
- confident copy
- editorial composition
- Ghanaian context
- consistent interaction patterns

The goal is not merely a beautiful website. It is a coherent Sender+ digital brand.
